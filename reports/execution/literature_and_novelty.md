## What the new results actually support

Hospital shows a precision/coverage tradeoff: label-assisted B preserves six extra reference cells without extra off-reference cells, while A has 18 extra reference hits and 453 off-reference alerts. Beers shows zero additional reference hits under every selector above the strong baseline, although A adds 770 off-reference cells. These are concrete counterexamples to “executability implies useful monitoring,” not a general theorem that LLM rules add no value. Conservative C can avoid workload by declining additional rules, which must be reported together with missed candidate coverage.

The journal candidate that describes equality but implements a sum-to-zero expression gives an inspectable implementation/meaning disagreement. The Beers alcohol bound illustrates unknown unit assumptions. Neither example supplies a population semantic-validity rate. The generic syntax reference also supplied numeric examples 0..10; anchoring is a possible confound, not a measured causal effect.

The proposed gap is therefore conditional: characterize when documentary/unlabelled qualification loses independently useful constraints or promotes unjustified ones under controlled changes of source contracts and units, with fixed real candidates and fair conventional controls. To earn a thesis contribution, show a failure boundary not already answered by Akella, Auto-Test, SPADE or Bayesian verification. Source representation, domain choice and a new dashboard alone do not meet that condition.

Current C cannot establish such a boundary alone: its certifiable documentary constraints are already contained in strong D, so redundancy rejection structurally forces zero acceptance there. Weak-control gains may simply restore known conventional checks. This sanity baseline is useful for interpreting claims, but its zero strong increment is not a new general negative finding or a sufficiently original thesis contribution.

A full closest-method implementation should be attempted only with enough primary method detail and legitimate resources. Reimplementing an inaccessible abstract and calling it Bayesian verification would be scientifically misleading. The present C is explicitly an interpretable baseline, not a novel estimator.
