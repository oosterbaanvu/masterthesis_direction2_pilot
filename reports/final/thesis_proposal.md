# Selecting generated data-quality rules under uncertain source semantics

**Provisional MSc Artificial Intelligence thesis proposal**

University of Groningen; nine-month internship with Avanade

21 October 2026 — supervisor decision document; conditional research direction

## 1. Problem and motivation

Enterprise teams receive new tables, changed deliveries and financial statements with inconsistent documentation. Conventional schema, business and reconciliation checks remain essential, but their coverage can lag behind new sources. Language models can propose executable checks from schemas, samples and domain descriptions. A proposed check can execute successfully while rejecting legitimate records, assuming an undocumented convention, duplicating an existing control or applying to the wrong reporting period. Conversely, rejecting uncertain checks can discard useful detections. The practical question is when a generated check deserves further review and promotion.

This thesis would investigate that selection decision, conditional on an existing control suite. Its intended engineering application is a small auditable source-onboarding and quality-monitoring service. The service would propose checks, preserve their provenance, report evidence and require explicit approval. It is a public-data demonstrator for an enterprise problem, not a production bank integration. Codex is the implementation agent and is not assumed to supply an experimental model API.

Two earlier pilots inform the direction. Pilot 1 investigated simulation-based detector selection and exposed dependence on the conventional baseline, uncertain natural-error references and limited transfer from injected errors. Pilot 2 established a reproducible control/service pipeline, audited public financial benchmarks and rescored published predictions. Those results are useful infrastructure and negative evidence; neither pilot currently establishes the proposed rule-selection hypothesis. This proposal preserves both and asks whether a tightly bounded continuation is scientifically justified.

## 2. Scientific context and unresolved question

The broad idea is already well studied. LeDQeR generates Great Expectations checks from identified dirty tuples, tests them and uses label-dependent precision/coverage selection. Even its JustDirty variant begins with a known erroneous tuple. Akella and colleagues use statistical inliers, domain information, rule enrichment, semantic conflict resolution and rubric filters before generating validators. LLM-DQR includes SQL generation, deduplication and execution repair. Auto-Test learns reusable semantic-domain constraints, controls false-positive behavior and studies unseen-table augmentation. SPADE selects assertions for LLM pipeline outputs using labelled examples and prompt histories. Deequ, dbt and Great Expectations provide mature verification precedents.

A Bayesian-inspired candidate-verification article published online on 20 October 2026 is especially close. Its publisher abstract describes shared candidate sets, semantic priors, SQL violation evidence and false-promotion comparisons. The full method remains inaccessible from this environment. It could substantially overlap with the proposed evidence layer. Therefore neither financial application nor a renamed filtering stage is claimed as novel.

The provisional research question is:

> Under documented and undocumented source changes, when does label-free qualification of a frozen set of generated relational checks falsely promote unjustified rules or discard checks with incremental labelled coverage, relative to technical filtering, contract matching and a label-assisted coverage reference?

The proposed contribution is an empirical reliability and limitation study, rather than a new rule-generation algorithm. It would separate semantic validity, source applicability and actual incremental detection; hold candidates and installed controls fixed; and evaluate selection across genuinely separated source groups. Full comparison with the closest work is an early decision gate. If those studies already answer the question adequately, this direction must be narrowed further or discontinued.

## 3. What the feasibility work establishes

The original Pilot 2 results remain preserved on their own branch. Its non-LLM evaluation includes controlled cases, qualified dirty/clean reference comparisons, a structural SEC audit and rescoring of 24 released model prediction artifacts. These are historical results, not new generations or proof that a qualification method works.

The new phase ran the original LeDQeR evaluator, scorer, greedy selector and prompt renderer using Great Expectations 1.9.1 in an isolated locked environment. Fifty-seven handwritten test expectations executed across Flights, Beers, Hospital and Tax; the original and independent confusion counts agree. The handwritten provenance is explicit. The exercise demonstrated both execution compatibility and the possibility that rejecting imprecise checks loses labelled detections, but it does not reproduce LeDQeR's model results. The official repository's inspected full history contains no released generated-rule responses. Model generation remains unexecuted pending the user's provider choice.

A bounded financial adapter now represents signed linear relationships over explicit row and column coordinates, with accounting-negative parsing, currency/scale, rounding tolerance and evidence. Four proposed segment-total checks on a development SEC table satisfy their displayed arithmetic. All remain pending independent semantic review; none is promoted. This distinguishes an arithmetic implementation from an independently verified financial rule sample.

The new phase also acquired and hashed 147 official FinRule-Bench files. The release contains useful provided-principle tasks, but its cashflow table count differs from the paper, some multi-rule identifiers are duplicated or unlabelled, and upstream verification logs contain substantial failures. These are resource-qualification findings, not model accuracy. FinRule cannot yet be treated as unquestioned semantic gold. The current task also lacks the newly configured Kaggle token, so authentic BAF and Give Me Some Credit downloads remain unavailable here. Those optional datasets do not block the scientific design.

## 4. Experimental design

The central experiment compares selectors on the **same frozen, genuine model-generated candidates**. Generation receives only permitted schema, source documentation, an unlabelled development sample and existing-control descriptions. Targets, dirty/clean references, error annotations and final test data are excluded. Provider/model revision, prompts, response hashes, request parameters, sampling, repairs, runtime and token costs are recorded. Invalid generations remain in denominators. Repeated prompts and generations quantify within-source variability; they are not independent domains.

Arm A applies technical filtering: restricted constructor syntax, schema compatibility and successful execution. Arm B faithfully adapts LeDQeR's labelled greedy coverage, using development labels only, and is explicitly a label-assisted reference. Arm C starts with an auditable contract-matching baseline: source evidence frozen before generation, a trusted executable-derived signature, exact applicability scope, unit/missing conventions and format-invariance evidence. Missing evidence permits abstention; documented contradictions cause rejection. Arm D adds no rules to the identical installed controls. An applicable label-free baseline from the closest Bayesian work must be included after its full method is obtained; reconstructing it from an abstract is insufficient.

Contract matching is intentionally transparent and is not itself claimed as a new algorithm. A complete conventional suite may already implement every documented constraint, leaving no incremental benefit. Incompletely documented semantics may prevent justified acceptance of additional checks. This tradeoff is a principal object of study, rather than an assumption that more conservative selection is always better. If human candidate-specific entailment labels are introduced, that comparator changes information tier and must not be described as label-free.

Data have distinct roles. UCI Credit, Bank Marketing and Wine support controlled corruption and convention tests without claiming natural DQ gold. Qualified Raha/Jung pairs support reference-agreement and representation analyses. Independently reviewed financial samples support rule validity and applicability. SEC provides conditional logical-participant localization, while FinRule provides conditional reasoning with stated principles. Fraud/default targets and algorithmic repairs are never error labels. These tracks are reported separately.

The independent unit is a source group, with financial periods and corrupted variants clustered by issuer or canonical table family. A prospective target is approximately 40 eligible groups; this is a feasibility target, not a power calculation or 40 independent domains. A four-source micro-pilot must first establish candidate yield, annotation reliability, workload and variability. Development, validation and confirmation groups are frozen before inference. The existing 210 reserved SEC bases remain untouched. Already inspected FinRule material is exploratory and cannot be relabelled retrospectively as pristine confirmation data.

## 5. Independent evaluation and validity

Two independent reviewers would judge rule validity, applicability and convention support, blinded to model identity and selector decision. They would cite source documentation or original financial context and could answer CANNOT_JUDGE. Initial judgments are frozen before consensus. The study reports disagreement, missing-evidence frequency, raw agreement and an appropriate chance-corrected statistic. No simulated human judgment or secondary LLM opinion substitutes for independent labels. If only one reviewer is available, that limitation restricts the resulting claims.

Rule meaning and detection labels are separate. A semantically valid check can yield no additional observed error beyond existing controls. A technically valid check can generate many unverified alerts. Useful-rule rejection is therefore evaluated against a predefined counterfactual: a valid, applicable candidate that adds at least one adjudicated target error beyond the baseline on eligible cases. Both accepted and rejected candidates must be reviewed; reviewing only accepted rules would hide lost opportunities.

The co-primary outcomes are incremental labelled detections beyond installed controls, averaged across eligible source groups, and false promotion of independently invalid/inapplicable candidates. They are accompanied by useful-rule rejection and lost detections, acceptance/abstention coverage, additional unadjudicated findings, execution failures, runtime and cost. Unlabelled alerts are workload, not automatically false positives. Fixed review-budget performance is secondary. Controlled injected-cell recovery and SEC logical-participant localization have different meanings and are not pooled.

Paired group-level comparisons and cluster bootstrap intervals are used when the independent group count supports them. With only four micro-pilot sources, raw per-source outcomes take precedence over precise-looking population intervals. Issuer clustering and dependence across source variants are explicit. Exclusions for ambiguous identities, unsupported relations and misalignment are predefined and reported with coverage. Source-change experiments distinguish harmless formatting, changed semantics and absent or misleading documentation. Thresholds are chosen on development evidence and sensitivity analysis is fixed before final labels are inspected.

## 6. Resources, feasibility and practical artifact

The recommended first API experiment uses OpenAI GPT-4.1 mini with a verified dated revision if available, because it offers low-cost structured/code output and straightforward non-reasoning token accounting. A stronger GPT-5.4 mini comparator is optional after the pilot. Provider launch prices were read and match a pinned current catalogue; current account pricing must be checked before execution. At conservative caps of 20,000 input and 2,000 output tokens per call, 48 GPT-4.1-mini calls cost at most approximately US$0.54 before tax at those rates. A proposed pilot cap is US$2 and 60 total attempts. A two-model, 240-calls-per-model experiment with 20% retry allowance is approximately US$10.14, with a proposed US$15 ceiling. No paid calls are currently authorised.

Free/local alternatives are real but have tradeoffs. Paper-faithful Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B AWQ4, GLM-4.7-Flash AWQ4 and Gemma-4-31B FP8 require a suitable inference stack and memory; this machine has no NVIDIA device. Qwen3 is a strong reproduction option if a university GPU is available. A smaller Qwen2.5-Coder-7B CPU model is a different model with unmeasured DQ quality. Gemini 2.5 Flash has a verified paid tariff and a free tier, but free-tier product-improvement terms and quotas restrict its suitability. No endpoint credential is configured, and public datasets still require transmission-rights review.

Independent annotation is likely the larger resource dependency. Four candidates per 240 calls create up to 960 candidates before deduplication. Two four-minute reviews per candidate would require roughly 128 reviewer-hours, excluding detection labels and disagreements. This is an explicit workload scenario; the micro-pilot must measure actual throughput and reduce scope if necessary. More API calls cannot solve unavailable independent semantic evidence.

The artifact preserves onboarding, contracts, conventional checks, descriptive monitoring, proposed rules, technical qualification, explicit lifecycle approval, evidence-bearing findings and audit history. The financial adapter remains separate until reviewed. Existing local actor strings are not enterprise identity or authorization. No private Rabobank systems or user-interface expansion are required for the contribution.

## 7. Schedule, decision and expected contribution

The first two weeks resolve the closest-work comparison, data rights and reviewer commitment. Weeks three and four run the authorised micro-pilot and decide whether source meaning and annotation effort permit a fair comparison. Months two and three establish the final preregistered design and source/candidate freeze. Months four and five execute selection comparisons and independent review; month six examines source change and prompt robustness. Month seven performs confirmation/error analysis. Months eight and nine complete the thesis and reproducibility/service handover.

The present verdict is **CONDITIONAL GO for this bounded feasibility continuation**, not unconditional commitment to Pilot 2. Proceed to the full study only if the supervisor confirms a distinct research question, independent labels and source groups are feasible, and the real-candidate comparison is interpretable. A carefully established negative result can be a valid contribution; an unexecuted core comparison cannot demonstrate the hypothesis. If these gates fail, reassess both pilots on scientific validity rather than defaulting to Pilot 1 because it contains more experiments.
